# Business case for a software swarm brain for small USVs protecting subsea infrastructure

Research date: 6 November 2026. All figures carry currency and year. "Real procurement" means a signed contract or order. "Demo" means a trial, showcase or statement of intent. Items marked (read via search text) come from search-result text because the page itself could not be fetched. Team estimates appear only under Inferences and are labelled.

## 1. Who operates and pays in Denmark, and who else buys in the Baltic?

### Takeaway
Denmark has no separate coast guard: Navy Command holds surveillance, environmental response and search and rescue, so one buyer covers all three applications EDTH names. The closest live money is the DKK 4bn Navy Plan (2025), and the only signed Baltic USV-surveillance deal found is Sweden's October 2026 data-service contract; Denmark's Saildrone work is still a trial with a follow-on contract being written.

### Cited Findings

**Danish division of labour**
- Navy Command (under Defence Command Denmark) is the competent authority for maritime safety including vessel traffic management, monitoring and surveillance, environmental protection and response, search and rescue, and ship casualty assistance — [EMSA country profile](https://portal.emsa.europa.eu/web/dona/country-profiles/denmark/competent-authorities-for-coast-guard-functions)
- The same profile gives ship security to the Danish Maritime Authority (Søfartsstyrelsen), port security to the Danish Civil Aviation and Railway Authority, fisheries inspection to the Danish Fisheries Agency, and customs and smuggling to the Danish Customs Agency — [EMSA country profile](https://portal.emsa.europa.eu/web/dona/country-profiles/denmark/competent-authorities-for-coast-guard-functions)
- The police hold national border security, there is no national border guard, and at sea the police use Royal Danish Navy vessels as a platform plus maritime interception teams in all 12 districts; the Navy runs surveillance of territorial waters including radar and commands Home Guard vessels in more than 20 flotillas — [Baltic Sea Region Border Control Cooperation](https://www.bsrbcc.org/Webs/BSRBCC/EN/03_Member_States/member_states_node.html). This conflicts with EMSA, which lists Customs for maritime border control — [EMSA](https://portal.emsa.europa.eu/web/dona/country-profiles/denmark/competent-authorities-for-coast-guard-functions)
- The Navy describes a Navy Command with a National Maritime Operations Centre and three squadrons based mainly at Frederikshavn and Korsør — [Forsvaret](https://forsvaret.dk/en/organisation/navy)
- Procurement sits with the Ministry of Defence's acquisition agency: press calls it DALO ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/09/24/with-denmark-saildrone-logs-first-flag-registration-for-naval-surveillance-drone/) and [Janes](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/defence/ddac-2026-saildrone-planning-to-build-1015-voyager-usvs-per-year) use it), and Danish sources call it Forsvarsministeriets Materiel- og Indkøbsstyrelse, FMI — [Ritzau](https://via.ritzau.dk/pressemeddelelse/14357148/nyt-forsvarsprogram-skal-skabe-fremtidens-danske-forsvarsvirksomheder)

### Inferences
- In Denmark, say "Navy Command" or "the Navy and Marine Home Guard", not "coastguard". The EDTH scenario's "coastguard unit" is the Navy in Danish terms.
- Navy Command is a strong single buyer because surveillance, environmental response and rescue sit in one organisation. The same fleet can be pitched across all three EDTH applications without a new budget owner.
- The Marine Home Guard looks like the most natural small-boat user: 21 new vessels, coastal tasks, flotillas. No contact or interest is known, so present this as a hypothesis.
- Sweden bought through NORDEFCO, which suggests other Nordic buyers could use similar routes. The Finnish statement fits that, but no document says so.
- The Yle item is probably 2026: its article number falls well after Yle pieces from December 2024. Confirm before quoting a date.

### Gaps
- Whether the Danish Saildrone trial was paid, and at what price.
- Whether the Navy Plan's critical-infrastructure vessel has been contracted. I found no signed contract.
- Which share of the Navy Plan goes to "other autonomous units".
- JRCC Denmark's exact place in the structure and Søfartsstyrelsen's role in authorising uncrewed vessels: no source found.
- No Finnish Border Guard or Swedish Coast Guard USV procurement found. Polish Border Guard, German Federal Police and Estonian PPA buying were not established.
- A figure of 18 planned German USVs appeared in search text but could not be tied to a source. Do not use it.
- Estonia's reported EUR 70m uncrewed prototype comes from an [ERR piece](https://news.err.ee/1608186910/navy-exploring-uncrewed-vessels-options-for-2030s) that looks several years old. Treat it as stale.

## 3. Who else pays, and what does a subsea fault cost?

### Takeaway
Owners carry the loss: EUR 50–60m for EstLink 2 and about EUR 35–40m for Balticconnector, and that money may not be recoverable. Public evidence shows them monitoring and sharing data, not paying for surface patrols, so they are the second customer and a strong reference, not the first buyer.

**Rules of thumb and consequences**
- Fibre repair about USD 0.5–1m per incident, power cable repair USD 10–100m; about 200 faults a year globally, 86% from fishing and anchoring — [Submarine Networks](https://www.submarinenetworks.com/en/nv/insights/statistics-on-subsea-cable-fault-and-repair). This is an industry aggregator, so use it as an order of magnitude only.
- Consumer power cost rose 10% during an earlier outage, per an S&P headline — [S&P Global, January 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/electric-power/012025-interview-estlink-2-outage-likely-to-impact-final-estonian-consumers---elering). I found no measured figure for the 2024–25 outage.
- Elering funds its share through congestion fees, and Elering and Fingrid planned to sue the owner — [offshore-energy.biz](https://offshore-energy.biz/shipowner-to-be-taken-to-court-over-damage-of-finland-estonia-subsea-link)
- In October 2025 the Helsinki District Court dismissed the Eagle S charges for lack of jurisdiction, because the acts took place outside Finnish territorial waters, and dismissed related damages claims. The ruling was appealable — [Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2025/10/03/finland-dismisses-case-over-baltic-cable-cuts-a90705); [World Ports](https://www.worldports.org/finnish-court-throws-out-eagle-s-cable-cutting-case)
- A Swedish prosecutor found the Vezhen ruptured the Sweden–Latvia cable by accident, and most Baltic outages since 2022 were caused by civilian ships dragging anchors — [Reuters via Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2025/02/24/813202.htm)

**What owners actually do**
- Energinet monitors cables and protection zones with 10–15 data sources including AIS and SCADA. In February 2022, historical AIS tied a vessel to anchor damage and the MMSI went to authorities. Repairs take one to six months, and liable operators have paid "several millions" — [Grafana case study](https://grafana.com/success/energinet/)
- Litgrid and the Lithuanian armed forces signed a cooperation agreement in January 2025 to share information on NordBalt; no financial terms are reported — [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2459548/lithuania-s-navy-steps-up-nordbalt-cable-protection-amid-reports-of-damage-attempts)
- Arelion owns about 17 regional cables and Elisa about three — [JSIS](https://jsis.washington.edu/news/baltic-sea-undersea-cable-security/). Elisa is reported testing threat-detection technology — [Pulse, low-grade aggregator](https://www.pulse.bot/telecom/news/elisa-tests-technology-that-could-detect-undersea-cable-threats-before-damage-occurs-31d1f46f-6b49-4729-9e40-995d7919f72d/)
- Crewed guard vessels are an existing private spend: Clarksons puts smaller support vessels at EUR 4,000–8,000 a day, with guard vessels at the low end (2023) — [Clarksons](https://www.clarksons.com/home/news-and-insights/2023/essential-vessels-for-offshore-windfarm-construction-and-operations/). One broker listing shows EUR 2,200–3,000 a day — [GRS listing](https://motorboats.apolloduck.com/boat.phtml?id=631135)

### Inferences
- The strongest business number is EUR 50–60m for one fault, and the second strongest is that the owner may not recover it. Pair them.
- Keep the claim to detection, evidence and warning. A 2–3 m boat cannot stop a tanker. The evidence value is real: Energinet's AIS record led to a compensation path, and an AIS-off vessel is the case AIS alone cannot cover.
- Do not say "sabotage" as fact. Many cases are accidents, and the pitch works either way because a dragged anchor costs the same.
- The commercial substitute for offshore wind and cable owners is a crewed guard vessel at EUR 4,000–8,000 a day, not a frigate. That is a natural later market, unproven for now.
- Insurance: I would not put insurers on the slide. The only evidence is that Balticconnector was insured.

**Deployment story for the deck**
1. Boat maker or operator bolts on the box. It talks MAVLink to the existing autopilot, which keeps its own safety behaviour (return-to-launch, loiter).
2. The swarm software runs on the box. Boats talk by mesh, with LTE near shore and satcom where needed.
3. One operator console on shore approves and vetoes. Output feeds the customer's existing operations room.
4. Boats launch from a harbour or a Home Guard vessel, patrol a shift, return and charge. Shifts overlap.
5. Over-the-air updates are signed. Each boat carries a licence key. Logs give an evidence trail for later claims.

**"Mass-manufacturable" for software plus a box**
- Software copies cost almost nothing, so scale is limited by test and support, not production.
- Every release runs through simulation in CI (ArduPilot SITL), which is the software equivalent of a production line.
- The box uses off-the-shelf parts, and a European EMS firm assembles and tests it. Kitron shows that defence-autonomy EMS work exists in Europe. No relationship exists, so word it as "EMS firms such as Kitron" only if the team is comfortable, or "Nordic EMS firms".
- One box fits many hulls, and the same software runs on Otter-class boats, BlueBoat demos, or a maker's own hull. This is what makes it scale faster than boats.
- Scale risks: radio supply and export licences, secure boot, and proving that no restricted-origin parts are inside. Avoid Chinese radios and hulls for defence customers.

## 7. Which business model is most credible, and what is a realistic first pilot?
