## What separates winners from the rest, and what mistakes are named?

### Takeaway
The clearest public signals are a working prototype shaped by operator feedback, a technical claim with a number on it, and a credible route to deployment. Three of the seven scored criteria concern manufacturing and market, and the team plan's 3-minute pitch currently gives them no time.

### Cited Findings

**The scoring system**
- 5-7 jurors covering angles such as Ukrainian military, European military, industry, VC and technical experts, chosen on Sunday morning. Each scores 0-10 on seven equal criteria: relevant problem, feasibility, originality, mass-manufacturability, business opportunity, size of opportunity, pitch. The highest total does not automatically win: scores guide a debate. Teams get anonymised written feedback — Onboarding PDF p.4
- Tech.eu says prototypes are judged by military experts, defence companies and investors, Ukrainian representation is always on the panel, and every team gets written feedback — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/06/29/the-hackathon-that-become-europe-s-defence-startup-ecosystem/)

**How to answer the seven criteria for a simulated swarm**

| Criterion | Weak answer | Stronger answer |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant problem | Baltic is important | One named user, one watch area, and a dated list of incidents described accurately |

## How should the team run a 3-minute pitch, the live moment and the fallback?

### Takeaway
Live demos are allowed, but I found no evidence of a judge interacting during a pitch, so this would be unproven. Keep it to one word from a juror and about 30 seconds of screen time, with a hard stop and a recorded fallback for every choice, and only if the organisers confirm the team can run its own machine.

### Cited Findings
- Format: 3-minute pitch plus 1-2 jury questions, strictly cut off, one team straight after another for roughly 1.5-2 hours, after which the jury retreats — Onboarding PDF p.3. A Milan agenda listed 3 minutes plus 2 of questions — [Luma Milan 2025](https://luma.com/edth-2025-milan)
- Live or recorded demos are both allowed — [Hackathon FAQ, Paris 2026](https://events.eurodefense.tech/paris-2026/hackathon-faq)
- The deck is uploaded as PDF or PPTX, with the demo as an uploaded video linked in the deck, and the organisers say they click the link. Late uploads may not be pulled from their server — Onboarding PDF p.2. This suggests the organisers run the deck from their own setup.
- The deck must cover the problem, the technical solution, deployment and mass manufacture, and what was achieved during the hackathon — Onboarding PDF p.3
- Suggested order: problem, feasibility, technical complexity, innovation, business, market, team, demo. The guide's three tips are to tell a story, speak clearly and end with a call to action. The story frame is for a person doing an activity, we built a thing, which keeps them alive or stops the enemy, and costs less — Onboarding PDF p.3
- Field demonstrations have happened on Demo Day: Prague 2026 held it at an airfield with drone demonstrations — [EY](https://www.ey.com/cs_cz/newsroom/2026/05/vitezem-european-defense-tech-hackathon-se-stal-projekt-zvysujici-spolehlivost-bezpilotnich-systemu), [Luma Prague](https://luma.com/edth-2026-prague)
- Copenhagen 2025 ran 26 teams between a 13:00 pitch start and a 14:30 ceremony — [Luma 2025](https://luma.com/edth-2025-copenhagen), [EDTH recap](https://eurodefense.tech/copenhagen-defense-tech-hackathon-2025/). Vilnius shortlisted 7 of 17 for the jury — [Resilience Media](https://www.resiliencemedia.co/p/dispatches-from-european-defense)
- Mentors can be jurors, since the FAQ reserves judging for military representatives, selected mentors and partner companies — [Hackathon FAQ, Paris 2026](https://events.eurodefense.tech/paris-2026/hackathon-faq)
- EDTH ran an online pitching workshop led by Antoine Leboyer (TUM Venture Labs) on structure, explaining technical ideas simply and avoiding common mistakes, but its content is not public — [EDTH event page](https://events.eurodefense.tech/invitation-to-online-pitching-workshop-with-antoine-leboyer)

### Inferences

**Recommendation.** Keep the judge-picks-a-failure moment, because it turns "does it work?" into proof, but make it conditional on logistics.

**Pitch running order, 3:00.** The demo sits early, so a failure still leaves time for the scored business material. Speaking room is about 350 words at normal pace. This keeps the plan's sequence, but cuts the live break from 50 to 35 seconds, the result from 50 to 30, the limits from 25 to 20 and the ask from 25 to 10. The opening gains 5 seconds, and 50 seconds go to manufacturing, business, market and team.

| Time | Beat | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:20 | "For the one operator watching 25 by 10 km of Baltic cable, we built..." | Problem, story |
| 0:20-0:35 | Why now: incidents, Nordics now buying USV surveillance as a service | Problem |
| 0:35-1:10 | **Live moment.** Boats running. Ask a military juror by name: "Which boat do we lose?" One word. Click. Fleet repairs, gap appears on screen, then closes | Feasibility, pitch |
| 1:10-1:40 | Insight and number: repair on vs off across seeded runs, worst revisit age, operator actions | Technical, originality |
| 1:40-2:00 | What is real and what is simulated; what we changed after mentors pushed back | Feasibility |
| 2:00-2:35 | Standard boats and radios, per-hull cost; who pays, price per vehicle-day, market | Manufacturing, business, size |
| 2:35-2:50 | Team and what was built this weekend | Team |
| 2:50-3:00 | Named ask, using the plan's pilot: "If you run a patrol or a cable, talk to us today about one supervised tabletop pilot" | Call to action |

**Rules for the live moment**
- Use numbered boats and a single click. A juror says one word. If there is no answer within 3 seconds, the presenter chooses the relay boat, which is the most dramatic failure.
- Run the sim at high time compression so the repair is visible within about 20 seconds.
- Learn jurors' names at the session opening. They are not known before Sunday. Choosing the military juror adds credibility and a Ukrainian seat is likely.
- Hard rule: at 1:10 on the stopwatch the presenter moves on, whatever is on screen.
- The plan's watch-out stands: a judge-selected run is exploratory. Quote measured numbers only from fixed, seeded runs, and do not claim the live run as a result.
- Only offer links that exist at that moment. An arithmetic check in the project memory (6 Nov, not rechecked here, measured off the plan's not-to-scale map) put boat 04 about 18 km from shore against an 8 km radio range, which would leave no direct shore link to cut.
- The same check found the headline metric, worst revisit age, ties across modes in the 1800 s script at 3 m/s. Confirm the on-versus-off comparison actually separates before building the 1:10 beat around it.

**If a boat "fails" badly in the sim.** That is the product, not a failure: narrate it as the gap the operator would see. Real failure only means the software froze or crashed.

**Failure protocol (rehearse it).** Say one sentence, such as "That's the sim, here's the same failure recorded an hour ago," and cut to the clip within 5 seconds. No apology, no debugging, no silence. Always say it is a recording. Military and technical jurors will spot a fake, and a hidden switch would cost far more than a crash.

**Fallback ladder**
1. Live on the team's laptop, local sim, no venue internet.
2. Pre-recorded 20-second clips for **every** choice (each boat, the relay, one link), stored locally and one keypress away, so the juror's choice is still honoured.
3. The mandatory video linked in the deck, on the organisers' machine.
4. A static slide: repair on vs off.

**Stage-logistics branch.** Settle this by Saturday midday.
- Own laptop and HDMI allowed: run as above.
- Only the organisers' machine: drop the live pick from the pitch, show the video, state the seeded result, and offer the juror the pick during the questions if a laptop can be held up.
- Do not rely on venue Wi-Fi or on a hosted URL as the main path.

**Rehearsal.** At least ten full timed runs, including at least three with a forced crash, with a teammate holding a stopwatch. Print the running order. Do not bring handouts.

**Pitch-slot risk.** At 3 minutes plus questions, 1.5-2 hours fits about 18-24 teams. If this event has more teams, expect a shortlist, parallel rooms or shorter questions. If there is a shortlist, the deck and video may decide whether the team pitches at all, so they need to stand alone without the live moment.

### Gaps
- No source shows a juror operating or choosing anything in an EDTH pitch. No source describes a live demo failing on stage in a pitch, nor which share of pitches use video.
- Whether teams can use their own laptop at DTU is unknown.
- Timing evidence for a successful 3-minute pitch comes from EDTH's guide and my own reasoning, not from recordings. YouTube recap videos exist but were not reviewed.

## What should the team do in the first two hours of Friday evening?

### Takeaway
Find the challenge owner and an operator first, then use the two hours to test the user, the operator policy and the business case rather than the algorithm. End with a written decision, because mentors may also be Sunday's jurors.

### Cited Findings
- Friday is meant for arriving, team formation and alignment; Saturday and Sunday are for building — Onboarding PDF p.1
- Luma lists workshops, team formation and networking from 14:00, with hacking from 18:00 — [Luma](https://luma.com/edth-2026-copenhagen). Other 2026 events presented challenges and mentors at 17:00 — [Luma Karlskrona](https://luma.com/edth-2026-karlskrona), [Luma Prague](https://luma.com/edth-2026-prague)
- Sponsor challenges come with dedicated mentors for questions and equipment — Onboarding PDF p.1
- Mentors cover hard technical problems, military and field experience, VC and fundraising, legal, manufacturing and more. They are there to help, not to recruit or sell — Onboarding PDF p.2
- The mentor's four questions: what problem are you really solving, who is the user, does it work in the real world, is it novel, useful and mass-producible — Onboarding PDF p.2
- Frontline feedback can reset a project in minutes, and the teams that held on and adapted did better than those who dropped everything — [Resilience Media](https://www.resiliencemedia.co/p/dispatches-from-kyiv-and-lviv-ukraines)

### Inferences

**Start before the two hours if possible.** The team plan puts mentor talks at Fri 19:00-21:00, but the challenge and mentor presentation may come before hacking. Be in the room for it, and note every name attached to USVs, undersea infrastructure and autonomy. Ask whether hacking starts at 18:00 or 19:00.

**Two-hour plan (send people in pairs, one talking, one writing)**

| Minutes | Who | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0-15 | Organisers | Owner of 07, the mentor, stage logistics, hardware |
| 15-50 | Military, naval or Ukrainian operators | Reality of the user and the operator policy |
| 50-80 | Technical and autonomy mentors | Has it been tried, why might it fail, is a 2D sim credible |
| 80-100 | Investors and industry | Who pays, how it scales, what the market is |
| 100-120 | Team together | One-page decision: user, metric, policy, business line. Freeze the contract |

**If there is only time for three questions:** number 1 (who owns 07), number 3 (can we run our own laptop), and number 7 (shadow at once, or wait for approval). The first decides who to work with, the second decides the demo, and the third decides the design.

**Ask organisers first**
1. Is challenge 07 a sponsor challenge or an open problem statement? Who owns it, and who is its mentor?
2. Does each challenge or track have its own recognition? (DEEPDOCK's claim suggests this may be so.)
3. Can we use our own laptop and HDMI? Is a live demo possible in the 3 minutes, and who clicks?
4. How many teams pitch to the jury? Is there a shortlist or parallel rooms?
5. Is any USV, radio, water tank or ASTA access available, and is there real data?

**Ask operators, in priority order**
6. Who actually owns this mission in Danish waters, and who would approve what? Is "coastguard" the right user at all?
7. Should a boat start shadowing at once with a veto, or wait for approval? (This settles the plan's open policy conflict.)
8. What would you accept the swarm doing alone, and what must always need a human?
9. What do you see today on your screen, and what would you throw away from ours?
10. What actually kills a small USV out there: sea state, fouling, radio range, traffic?
11. What does a suspicious vessel really look like? Is AIS-off the main signal, or is it AIS-on and behaving strangely?
12. What may a small USV legally do near a vessel in these waters? Is shadowing all that is possible?

**Ask technical mentors**
13. Has this been tried, and is there one simple reason it fails?
14. Will you believe a 2D sim? Which single real element would change your mind: AIS replay, a real radio link, or ArduPilot?
15. What baseline would you trust for comparison?

**Ask investors and industry**
16. Who signs the contract, and from which budget?
17. Would you price per hull, per vehicle-day, or per area?
18. What would make this a company rather than a feature of a boat maker's product?
19. Who else in the room should we talk to tomorrow?

**Working habits**
- Show the scenario map rather than describe it, and ask for criticism directly.
- Treat each mentor as a possible juror: a good first impression on Friday may matter on Sunday.
- Keep a mentor log, in the form "Friday we assumed X, mentor Y said Z, we changed W". This feeds the iteration the guide wants under technical complexity and gives the pitch a strong line.
- If a mentor attacks the idea, spend a while finding what can be kept before pivoting, given what happened at Lviv.
- End with one go or no-go: if no operator will accept the user or the policy, change them on Friday, as the guide itself advises.
