## 1. What did the Baltic incidents since 2023 really look like?

### Takeaway
The real pattern is not a dark vessel loitering over a cable. It is a ship under way, usually with AIS on, slowing as an anchor drags, first noticed by the cable or pipeline owner, and reached by radio one to three hours later. Only Yi Peng 3 has a reported AIS gap. One first-instance conviction exists (Fitburg, 5 Nov 2026, not final). No incident in the list damaged a cable in Danish waters, though Yi Peng 3 transited and anchored there, and the Bornholm cable has its own older history.

**Signature analysts used afterwards**
- Fault time and position matched against AIS tracks. BT used this from 2006 ([ICPC bulletin](https://www.iscpc.org/documents/?id=139)). Crossing a cable at the moment it breaks shows possible cause, not proof ([TeleGeography](https://resources.telegeography.com/what-to-know-about-submarine-cable-breaks)).
- Speed loss and engine revolutions (Eagle S), plus slowing and turning (Vezhen), as cited above.
- Track oddities: U-turn (Eagle S), repeated crossings, drift, circles and AIS gap (Yi Peng 3), as cited above.
- Physical evidence: seabed drag track, recovered anchor, damaged anchor, windlass and securing gear ([judgment](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-14133-2025-INIT/en/pdf), [The Record](https://therecord.media/sweden-report-chinese-ship-baltic-sea-cable)).

**Danish waters.** The Bornholm cable between Sweden and Denmark was cut in 2004, 2010, 2013 and on 28 Feb 2022, the last by the tanker Samus Swan per Wikipedia ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Sea_underwater_infrastructure_incidents)). Yi Peng 3 passed under the Great Belt bridge ([TV 2](https://nyheder.tv2.dk/samfund/2024-11-19-soevaernet-foelger-kinesisk-skib-der-var-taet-paa-beskadigede-kabler)) and anchored in the Danish economic zone ([DR](https://www.dr.dk/nyheder/indland/danmark-maatte-se-paa-mens-kineserne-undersoegte-deres-eget-skib-det-er-en-meget)). Sweden and Denmark closed their Nord Stream investigations in 2024 without naming suspects ([Baird](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/marine-projects/marine-infrastructure/factbox-suspected-underwater-cable-sabotage-in-the-baltic-sea)).

**Base rate.** ICPC attributes about 70-80% of the roughly 150-200 yearly cable faults to accidental fishing and anchoring ([ICPC](https://www.iscpc.org/publications/icpc-viewpoints/charting-submarine-cables-is-critical-for-maritime-safety-and-infrastructure-protection/)).

### Inferences
- The challenge's AIS-off loiterer is a fair test case but not the historic signature. The plan's rule (no AIS match, under 1 kn, within 200 m, X minutes) would not have fired on Eagle S or Fitburg, which were moving. Yi Peng 3 is the only partial match. Say this openly; judges who know the cases will respect it.
- Add a second alert: AIS-on ship slows sharply or turns while crossing a charted cable corridor. Keep it explainable and call it an alert, not a detector.
- In the cases with a clear source, the first signal came from the cable or pipeline owner, not a patrol. The most realistic extra event is a "cable fault reported at km X" message that retasks a USV and lists AIS crossings near that time.
- Eagle S average ground speed was roughly 7.6 kn or less (90 km over about 6.4 h, my arithmetic, assuming the anchor was already down at 12:26). Yi Peng 3 was about 7 kn. The plan's 3 m/s is 5.8 kn, so a USV cannot stay with these ships. It can hold a stopped contact, or hand over to a boat further along the track.
- Departure from a Russian port is not a discriminator: Vezhen left Ust-Luga and was ruled an accident.

### Gaps
- Newnew Polar Bear total drag distance and speed. I recall a 180 km figure but found no source, so do not use it.
- Eagle S and Fitburg speeds in knots, and the Fitburg boarding time. Trial records would have them.
- Hong Kong trial outcome after February 2026. Nothing found in English or Chinese.
- Eagle S leave application to the Supreme Court. Nothing found.
- Any cable incident after 2 Jan 2026. Nothing found, but the search budget ended, so absence is weak evidence.

## 2. What did the Danish Navy do with Yi Peng 3, and what could it legally do?

### Takeaway
Denmark watched, stayed close and negotiated. It followed the ship from the Great Belt, kept a vessel near it at anchor for a month, and did not board. That is the closest real precedent for the demo, and the lesson is that keeping a vessel in view is the lawful default while everything further needs the flag state.

### Cited Findings
- The Danish Navy began following at the Great Belt ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Baltic_Sea_submarine_cable_disruptions)). TV 2's MarineTraffic data showed three Navy ships on 19 Nov ([TV 2](https://nyheder.tv2.dk/samfund/2024-11-19-soevaernet-foelger-kinesisk-skib-der-var-taet-paa-beskadigede-kabler)).
- Webcams showed Navy vessels loosely following in its wake, with HDMS Hvidbjørnen a few minutes behind ([gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/details-of-baltic-sea-cable-incident-remain-murky-as-danish-navy-shadows-chinese-vessel/)).
- A Danish pilot was reportedly aboard during the transit, based only on online reports ([gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/details-of-baltic-sea-cable-incident-remain-murky-as-danish-navy-shadows-chinese-vessel/)).
- About 20:00 CET on 19 Nov the ship left the corridor, slowed and anchored; HDMS Rota stayed near and HDMS Søløven arrived hours later ([gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/chinese-vessel-suspected-in-cable-incident-remains-stopped-with-danish-navy-alongside/)).
- TWZ quoted reports that Rota "detained" the ship ([TWZ](https://www.twz.com/news-features/danish-navy-shadows-chinese-cargo-ship-after-baltic-sea-cable-damage)). Wikipedia says the ship itself asked to anchor ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Baltic_Sea_submarine_cable_disruptions)). Treat "detained" as disputed.
- Danish Defence said only that it was present, and that it routinely follows ships through Danish waters ([TV 2](https://nyheder.tv2.dk/samfund/2024-11-19-soevaernet-foelger-kinesisk-skib-der-var-taet-paa-beskadigede-kabler), [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/chinese-vessel-suspected-in-cable-incident-remains-stopped-with-danish-navy-alongside/)).
- The ship lay in the Danish economic zone outside territorial waters. Danish authorities could not act or board without Chinese permission, and no state could order it to a port ([DR](https://www.dr.dk/nyheder/indland/danmark-maatte-se-paa-mens-kineserne-undersoegte-deres-eget-skib-det-er-en-meget)). It was reported less than 1 nm outside the territorial sea ([Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/wsj-chinese-ship-dragged-anchor-for-100-miles-and-cut-two-cables)).
- A Danish navy expert said there was no legal basis to stop it had it sailed on ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Baltic_Sea_submarine_cable_disruptions)). Foreign Minister Løkke Rasmussen said it could have weighed anchor and left ([DR](https://www.dr.dk/nyheder/indland/danmark-maatte-se-paa-mens-kineserne-undersoegte-deres-eget-skib-det-er-en-meget)).
- Denmark's Foreign Ministry hosted meetings with Finland, Sweden, Germany and China ([DR](https://www.dr.dk/nyheder/indland/danmark-maatte-se-paa-mens-kineserne-undersoegte-deres-eget-skib-det-er-en-meget)).
- On 19 Dec a joint team of 14 Chinese, 9 Germans, 6 Swedes, 3 Finns and 1 Dane boarded for about five hours. The ship left on 21 Dec ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Baltic_Sea_submarine_cable_disruptions)).
- Lott suggests Denmark might act against non-innocent passage in the straits, but that depends on reading the 1857 treaty with modern law ([Lieber Institute](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/baltic-sea-cable-cuts-ship-interdiction-c-lion1-incident/)).

### Inferences
- Lawful, repeatable actions in that case were: follow, observe, radio, record, coordinate with neighbours, and go diplomatic. A USV swarm supports the first four.
- Pitch sentence: "When a suspect ship reached Danish waters in 2024, the Navy's job was to keep it in view while others worked out what the law allowed. That is the job our boats do."

### Gaps
- Official Danish account of the Navy's orders, distances kept, or who authorised what. Danish Defence made almost no comment.
- Name of the Danish navy expert. The primary interview was not found.

## 3. How do real watch centres work, and what does supervision of several uncrewed vessels look like?

### Takeaway
Danish maritime watch functions sit under Naval Command, with civilian rule-making at the Danish Maritime Authority. The usable vocabulary is standardised: message markers (information, warning, advice, instruction) and alert priorities (alarm, warning, caution). No study proves one operator can safely supervise several vessels, so the demo should say it reduces load but not claim a ratio.

### Cited Findings

**Who does what in Denmark**
- The Joint Operations Centre is staffed around the clock and coordinates operations with the National Maritime Operations Centre (NMOC). NMOC is a Navy Command unit in Karup and carries out JRCC tasks ([Danish Defence](https://forsvaret.dk/en/organisation/joint-services/joint-operations-centre-joc)).
- JRCC is staffed 24/7 by four people: an air force officer and NCO, and a naval officer and NCO ([Danish Defence](https://forsvaret.dk/en/organisation/joint-services/joint-operations-centre-joc)).
- NMOC also operates the Maritime Assistance Service, per the EU coastguard directory (search extract, [EU Coast Guard Functions Forum](https://coastguard.europa.eu/national-authorities/denmark)). MAS covers ships in difficulty with no danger to life ([IMO A.950(23)](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/OurWork/Safety/Documents/Documents%20relevant%20to%20SAR/A%2023-Res.950%20-%20Maritime%20Assistance%20Services%20(Mas).pdf), search extract).
- The DMA is the competent VTS authority and Naval Command is the VTS provider ([DMA](https://www.dma.dk/safety-at-sea/safety-of-navigation/mandatory-ship-reporting-systems-msrs-and-vessel-traffic-services-vts/guidelines-on-vessel-traffic-services-vts-in-danish-waters)).
- Great Belt VTS is at Naval Base Korsør, call sign BELT TRAFFIC. Sector 1 uses VHF 74 and 16, sector 2 uses 11 and 16, with 10 as spare. Reporting applies at 50 GT and above, and pleasure craft under 15 m or 50 GT are exempt ([Danish Defence](https://forsvaret.dk/da/organisation/soevaernet/civile-opgaver/beltrep)).
- Sound VTS runs SOUNDREP jointly with Sweden, and VTS Fehmarn Belt is a temporary voluntary service ([DMA](https://www.dma.dk/safety-at-sea/safety-of-navigation/mandatory-ship-reporting-systems-msrs-and-vessel-traffic-services-vts/guidelines-on-vessel-traffic-services-vts-in-danish-waters)).
- VTS must keep traffic data and communications at least 90 days for mandatory reporting systems and 30 days for VTS, and report infringements to the DMA within 30 days ([DMA](https://www.dma.dk/safety-at-sea/safety-of-navigation/mandatory-ship-reporting-systems-msrs-and-vessel-traffic-services-vts/guidelines-on-vessel-traffic-services-vts-in-danish-waters)).

**VTS language and limits**
- VTS is defined as a service able to interact with traffic and respond to developing situations. It provides information or issues advice, warnings and instructions as needed ([IMO A.1158(32)](https://admin.dma.dk/Media/638539546076830031/A%2032-Res.1158%20-%20GUIDELINES%20FOR%20VESSEL%20TRAFFIC%20SERVICES%20(Secretariat).pdf)).
- Ships comply with VTS instructions unless contradictory safety or environmental reasons exist. Masters may have to report if they disregard one. Ultimate responsibility stays with the master ([IMO A.1158(32)](https://admin.dma.dk/Media/638539546076830031/A%2032-Res.1158%20-%20GUIDELINES%20FOR%20VESSEL%20TRAFFIC%20SERVICES%20(Secretariat).pdf)).
- SMCP has eight message markers; VTS uses seven: information, warning, advice, instruction, question, answer, request ([IALA G1089 revision](https://iala.int/content/uploads/2020/10/Revision-of-Guideline-1089.docx)). Current phraseology guidance is [IALA G1132:2022](https://shop.standards.ie/en-ie/standards/iala-g1132-2022-559182_saig_iala_iala_3211637).

**Alert discipline**
- Bridge alert management has four priorities: emergency alarm, alarm, warning, caution. A warning is not yet hazardous but may become so. A caution calls for awareness only and needs no acknowledgement ([MSC.302(87)](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/KnowledgeCentre/IndexofIMOResolutions/MSCResolutions/MSC.302(87).pdf)).
- An unacknowledged alarm's audible signal restarts after 30 s. An unacknowledged warning repeats or escalates within at most 5 minutes ([MSC.302(87)](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/KnowledgeCentre/IndexofIMOResolutions/MSCResolutions/MSC.302(87).pdf)).
- The stated purpose is to reduce cognitive load by avoiding redundant alarms ([MSC.302(87)](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/KnowledgeCentre/IndexofIMOResolutions/MSCResolutions/MSC.302(87).pdf)). Alert states include active-acknowledged and active-responsibility-transferred ([UK approval recommendation](https://www.gov.uk/marine-equipment-approved-recommendations/bridge-alert-management-uk21-slash-n004), search extract).

**Reports, logs and handover**
- The SAR situation report format is in IAMSAR Volume III Appendix D, per secondary training material ([SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/iamsarpptx/254425528)). The manual itself is sold, not free.
- Canadian SAR practice treats a transfer of mission responsibility as a formal event with timed, detailed log entries ([DFO](https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/library-bibliotheque/342659.pdf), search extract). That is national practice, not IAMSAR.
- Real handover points are visible in the cases. Finnish police took over the Eagle S lead at 18:40 on suspicion of a crime ([Police](https://poliisi.fi/en/-/eagle-s-tanker-to-move-to-international-waters-under-border-guard-s-control), search extract). Fitburg moved from the Gulf of Finland Coast Guard to Helsinki Police ([TWZ](https://www.twz.com/news-features/first-ship-seized-for-undersea-cable-cutting-since-natos-baltic-sentry-began)).
- Boarding sat at government level in one case: the Border Guard alerted PM Orpo, who approved a special forces boarding ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/eagle-s-how-yi-peng-3-and-newnew-polar-bear-wreaked-havoc-in-baltic-sea-60-minutes/)).
- A commentator on the MASS Code urges a decision-responsibility map: who may change the voyage plan, override automation, declare a comms emergency or authorise degraded operation ([Maritime Executive editorial](https://maritime-executive.com/editorials/the-mass-code)).

**Terms.** The US National Maritime Domain Awareness Plan has a Vessel of Interest Lexicon at Appendix F ([NMIO](https://nmio.ise.gov/Portals/16/Docs/NMDAP_Aug2025.pdf), search extract). An older US concept lists "Contact of Interest" alongside "Vessel of Interest" ([NMIO](https://nmio.ise.gov/Portals/16/Docs/071213mdaconops.pdf), search extract). I could not read the definitions.

**Supervision and workload**
- One proposal gave each operator about six ships at about 10 minutes per vessel, and noted the right number depends on traffic and workload ([AHFE paper](https://openaccess-api.cms-conferences.org/articles/download/978-1-958651-36-0_79)).
- A human factors review cites up to six vessels per operator and warns of carry-over errors when attention is split ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/9/4/385)).
- The Nippon Foundation centre was built to support four autonomous ships at once in 2025 trials ([Nippon Foundation](https://en.nippon-foundation.or.jp/news/articles/2024/20240718-102859.html)).
- A 2025 arXiv paper says it knows of no published robust one-operator, multi-vessel demonstration in realistic conditions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24470), search extract; the full PDF was too large to confirm).
- In UAV work, Cummings and Mitchell found operator capacity falls 36% when situation-awareness wait time is included, even under management by exception ([MIT](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/90280)). Related work cites 4-5 vehicles at flight control level and up to 12 under supervisory control ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00568/pdf)).
- One editorial argues staffing should follow credible worst-case demand, since a ratio can fail when several ships are affected at once ([Maritime Executive editorial](https://maritime-executive.com/editorials/the-mass-code)).
- Danish test: four Saildrone Voyagers over six months, reported as 170,000+ ships logged, 20,000+ nm and 92% uptime, with no named source ([Yacht](https://www.yacht.de/en/travel-and-charter/denmark/sailing-drones-efficient-surveillance-on-the-baltic-sea-soon-with-weapons/)). Saildrone Mission Control tracks operations 24/7 ([Saildrone](https://www.saildrone.com/news/saildrone-launches-the-future-of-maritime-surveillance-in-the-baltic-sea)).
- Dover and Singapore VTS reportedly rotate positions every 45 minutes ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/23/8004)). Night shifts raise fatigue, and fatigue tracks workload ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10111-025-00804-x), [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10140575/)).

### Inferences
Recommended screen vocabulary (the first three rows are sourced usage; the rest are proposals to test with a mentor):

| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| Information / Advice / Warning / Instruction | Notification, suggestion |
| Alarm / Warning / Caution | Critical / High / Low |
| Acknowledge (I have seen it) | Approve, when nothing is being decided |

| Contact of interest (a person marked it, with a reason) | Hostile, suspicious vessel |
| Vessel of interest (identified) | Bad actor, shadow fleet ship |
| Keeping in view / Holding contact | Hunting, chasing, intercepting |

- Keep one contact as one decision card. In a scene with low battery, link loss and a contact, use caution for the first two and warning for the contact.
- Re-alert an unanswered warning at 5 minutes, and escalate to alarm. That borrows a real standard and answers alarm fatigue directly.
- Say "one watch officer, five boats, tested in simulation" and never "one operator can run five boats".

## 4. What does the law allow when watching and shadowing a vessel?

### Takeaway
Watching is ordinary navigation and needs no special power. Stopping or boarding is the hard part. The Eagle S appeal ruling and the Fitburg verdict strengthen the right to try cable cases after the fact, but neither gives a coastal state a clear power to stop a foreign ship at sea. In the Danish straits the 1857 treaty makes interference harder still.

### Cited Findings

**Territorial sea**
- Ships have innocent passage, which must be continuous and expeditious; stopping and anchoring count only if incidental to ordinary navigation or forced ([UNCLOS Arts 17-18](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part2.htm)).
- Acts aimed at interfering with communications systems, or activity not directly related to passage, are prejudicial ([UNCLOS Art 19(2)(k)-(l)](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part2.htm)).
- The coastal state may legislate to protect cables, must not hamper innocent passage, and may take necessary steps to prevent non-innocent passage ([UNCLOS Arts 21, 24, 25](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part2.htm)).
- Criminal jurisdiction aboard a passing ship is limited, with exceptions including consequences extending to the coastal state ([UNCLOS Art 27](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part2.htm)).

**EEZ**
- Coastal rights cover resources and listed matters. Other states keep navigation and cable-laying freedoms, with due regard ([UNCLOS Arts 56, 58](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part5.htm)).
- Safety zones around installations are capped at 500 m ([UNCLOS Art 60(5)](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part5.htm)). Boarding powers in Art 73 concern living resources ([UNCLOS Art 73](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part5.htm)).
- Flag-state jurisdiction is the rule ([UNCLOS Art 92](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part7.htm)). Article 97 limits penal proceedings after collisions and other incidents of navigation to the flag or national state ([UNCLOS Art 97](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part7.htm)).
- The right of visit covers piracy, slave trade, unauthorised broadcasting, statelessness and hidden nationality, not cable damage ([UNCLOS Art 110](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part7.htm)).
- Article 113 requires states to criminalise wilful or negligent cable damage by their own ships and nationals ([UNCLOS Art 113](https://www.un.org/depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part7.htm)).
- Lott notes the Balticconnector pollution route to hot pursuit does not transfer to cable cuts, and piracy is a weak fit. Consent boarding and the 1884 Convention allow little more than checks of papers and questions. Necessity remains a debated idea ([Lieber Institute](https://lieber.westpoint.edu/baltic-sea-cable-cuts-ship-interdiction-c-lion1-incident/)).

**Eagle S and Fitburg**
- The District Court held on 3 Oct 2025 that Articles 97 and 113 blocked Finnish law, and did not assess the conduct ([judgment](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-14133-2025-INIT/en/pdf)).
- The Court of Appeal (R 16/2025/2572, 27 Aug 2026) treated the offence as also committed in Finland because consequences appeared there. It held Article 92 does not exclude that, and Article 113 only imposes a duty to criminalise ([LKOS](https://www.lkoslaw.fi/insights/params/post/5301548/anchor-cable-jurisdiction-the-eagle-s-appeal-and-unclos-article-97)).
- On Article 97 it held the first anchor loss was an incident, but alleged conduct after contact and over three further hours of dragging was not ([LKOS](https://www.lkoslaw.fi/insights/params/post/5301548/anchor-cable-jurisdiction-the-eagle-s-appeal-and-unclos-article-97)).
- Article 59 was used to settle the conflict with the Cook Islands. Ipsen praises the stage-by-stage reasoning, criticises the Article 59 step, and stresses that enforcement jurisdiction in the EEZ stays unaddressed ([EJIL:Talk](https://www.ejiltalk.org/has-the-eagle-landed-criminal-jurisdiction-for-submarine-cable-damage-in-the-eez-at-the-helsinki-court-of-appeal/)).
- Another author argues dragging is not "of navigation" at all, whatever the intent ([Völkerrechtsblog](https://voelkerrechtsblog.org/interpreting-treaties-in-domestic-courts/), search extract).
- The Fitburg court rejected the jurisdiction challenge on impact in Finland ([AP via Balita](https://www.balitangmarino.com/post/finland-jails-captain-and-boatswain-for-intentional-anchor-dragging)). The damage was in Estonia's EEZ ([Police](https://poliisi.fi/en/-/fitburg-vessel-to-move-to-international-waters-under-border-guard-s-control)). Prosecutors also alleged some damage in Finnish territorial waters ([Yle](https://yle.fi/a/74-20245189)).
- In both cases the ship was taken under control after moving into Finnish territorial waters ([TWZ](https://www.twz.com/news-features/first-ship-seized-for-undersea-cable-cutting-since-natos-baltic-sentry-began), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Estlink_2_incident)). Whether that entry was voluntary is disputed, and the District Court called it irrelevant ([judgment](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-14133-2025-INIT/en/pdf)).

**Danish straits**
- The 1857 treaty ended the Sound Dues for a lump sum, signed 14 March 1857 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Convention_of_1857)).
- Denmark's position is that it is a long-standing convention under UNCLOS Art 35(c), so Part III transit rules do not apply. Customary law supplements it ([Gorrissen Federspiel](https://gorrissenfederspiel.com/en/passing-through-russian-vessels-in-the-danish-straits/)).
- Art I(1) bars detention or hindrance, whatever the pretext, as cited by Lott ([EJIL:Talk comments](https://www.ejiltalk.org/why-denmark-cant-block-dark-tankers/)). Lott reports that only the US principally argues for transit passage there, and Siig says Denmark would likely read the treaty and UNCLOS together ([EJIL:Talk comments](https://www.ejiltalk.org/why-denmark-cant-block-dark-tankers/)).
- Denmark cannot block or detain tankers in transit on mere suspicion, but vessels lingering at anchor may fall outside transit protection ([EJIL:Talk](https://www.ejiltalk.org/why-denmark-cant-block-dark-tankers/)). Inspection and detention can rest on clear pollution grounds, or on flag-state consent ([Gorrissen Federspiel](https://gorrissenfederspiel.com/en/passing-through-russian-vessels-in-the-danish-straits/)).
- Since 2025 the DMA has inspected tankers anchored off Skagen that cannot be considered in innocent passage. Scope widened in Oct 2025. In 2025 it inspected 122 ships and detained five ([gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/denmark-tightens-oversight-of-shadow-fleet-tankers-in-strategic-baltic-gateway/), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/14/law-of-the-sea-comes-to-life-in-the-hunt-for-sanctioned-russian-oil)). Siig says closing the straits is not under consideration ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/14/law-of-the-sea-comes-to-life-in-the-hunt-for-sanctioned-russian-oil)).

**Danish cable zone**
- Danish rules set a 200 m protection zone on each side of a charted cable or pipeline. Anchoring, sand suction, stone fishing and bottom-towed gear are banned; breach is a fine; the basis is order no. 939 of 27 Nov 1992 ([DMA page](https://www.soefartsstyrelsen.dk/SikkerhedTilSoes/Sejladssikkerhed/EntreprenoeropgaverSoes/sider/soekablerroerledningerhavbunden.aspx), search extract; the page returned 404 on direct fetch). [DKCPC](https://dkcpc.dk/introduktion-til-dkcpc/) explains the cable risk.
- ICPC prefers zones marked on charts and asks coast guards to contact vessels operating or drifting near cables. It also recommends AIS at all times with offences for disabling it ([ICPC best practices v1.2](https://www.iscpc.org/documents/?id=3733)).

**AIS-off is not itself proof of anything**
- AIS is required at 300 GT on international voyages and 500 GT for domestic cargo ships ([IUMI](https://iumi.com/news/iumi-eye-newsletter-december-2019/regulatory-framework-for-ais-going-dark-and-switching-off), search extract). Small leisure and fishing craft are generally exempt from carriage ([Dutch IMO summary](https://puc.overheid.nl/doc/PUC_1361_14/2), search extract).
- A master may switch off where operation might compromise safety or security, and must log it and report ([IMO A.1106(29)](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/OurWork/Safety/Documents/AIS/Resolution%20A.1106(29).pdf), search extract).
- The DMA warns AIS shows only ships that carry it, and can be wrong through bad input or faults ([DMA](https://www.dma.dk/safety-at-sea/navigational-information/ais-data)).

**After the incidents**
- EU: action plan Feb 2025; regional hubs proposed; cable security toolbox and €347m on 5 Feb 2026 ([Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_327/IP_26_327_EN.pdf)). The toolbox is not legally binding ([HPP](https://hpp.fi/news/the-eu-cable-security-toolbox-what-offshore-infrastructure-operators-need-to-know/)).
- In June 2026 the Commission funded two hubs. Finland leads the Baltic one, tested in MARSEC EU 2026 ([Council doc 15170/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-15170-2026-INIT/en/pdf)).
- The same paper records the Vihula MoU (May 2025), IRINI flag-verification boardings under Art 110 (six by Aug 2026), ATALANTA tasked likewise in July 2026, about 700 sanctioned vessels, and Directive (EU) 2025/811 requiring insurance certificates from ships in transit ([Council doc 15170/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-15170-2026-INIT/en/pdf)).
- NATO: Baltic Sentry began early 2025. A NATO official said it supported Finland with analysis in Fitburg and called it a national police case ([TWZ](https://www.twz.com/news-features/first-ship-seized-for-undersea-cable-cutting-since-natos-baltic-sentry-began)).
- Finland: a bill sent to Parliament on 10 Sep 2026 gives the Border Guard a duty to supervise undersea infrastructure in the territorial sea and EEZ, plus stop-and-inspect for suspected stateless vessels ([Finnish Government via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2026/09/mil-260910-fingov01.htm), search extract; [Dec 2025 draft](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/en/-/1410869/finnish-border-guard-s-powers-related-to-preparedness-to-be-strengthened-)). Passage not confirmed.
- Denmark: no new law found. A converted ship for undersea monitoring goes to Frederikshavn, due end 2026, with up to about 50 staff ([FMN](https://www.fmn.dk/da/nyheder/2026/forligskredsen-enig-om-geografisk-placering-af-kystmissilforsvaret-og-kapaciteten-til-kritisk-undersoisk-infrastruktur/)).
- Estonia considered a bill allowing ships to be sunk after evacuation ([The Record](https://therecord.media/estonia-bill-sink-ships-sabotaging-undersea-cables)). Status not confirmed.

### Inferences
- Nothing in these sources limits a vessel from staying within sight of another at a safe distance, provided it does not hamper passage. Keep the word "shadowing" for that meaning only.
- Screen rule: show the zone beside every contact, because the zone sets what can be asked for. Territorial sea: "Passage may not be innocent. Authority may consider action." EEZ: "Flag state consent needed." Danish strait: "1857 Treaty applies. Consult legal." These are prompts, not legal advice.
- The Fitburg and Eagle S rulings raise the value of recording what happened after first contact, because the appeal court's reasoning turned on conduct over time.
- The plan's 200 m trigger happens to match the Danish protection zone. Use the phrase "inside the 200 m cable protection zone", and note that it only bans anchoring and bottom gear. A stopped ship not at anchor may break no rule, so the screen needs an "Anchor: not seen" field.
- Waters between Danish islands may be internal waters or territorial sea, depending on baselines, and the 1857 regime still applies to the Belts. The demo should not state a zone for a schematic map unless the team has plotted one.

### Gaps
- Primary text of the 1857 treaty and Denmark's UNCLOS declaration were not read. The ODIL articles on straits and EEZ corridors refused fetch.
- Danish baselines for a Great Belt scene, and whether the 200 m ban applies in the EEZ.
- Sweden's and Germany's 2026 law changes. Nothing confirmed.
- Article 111(2) wording was only seen in summary, and Articles 95-96 on warships and state vessels were not checked.

## 5. What rules apply to the USVs themselves?

### Takeaway
COLREGs apply to anything that counts as a vessel, and how they apply to small uncrewed craft is unsettled. The MASS Code is now adopted but non-mandatory and aimed at cargo ships. In Denmark a civil trial needs prior permission from the Danish Emergency Management Agency, at least four weeks ahead, while defence activities fall outside that order.

### Cited Findings
- The MASS Code was adopted as MSC.595(111) in May 2026, effective 1 July 2026, non-mandatory. It targets SOLAS cargo ships, with states encouraged to apply it to smaller MASS as far as practicable ([IMO](https://www.imo.org/en/mediacentre/hottopics/pages/autonomous-shipping.aspx)).
- MSC 112 in Dec 2026 works on an experience-building phase; a mandatory code is expected by 1 July 2030, in force 1 Jan 2032 ([IMO](https://www.imo.org/en/mediacentre/hottopics/pages/autonomous-shipping.aspx)). DNV notes the timeline may be revisited ([DNV](https://www.dnv.com/news/2026/imo-mcs-111-new-mass-code-adopted/), search extract).
- The master keeps overall responsibility at all times, and remote operations centres should be assessed, certified and run under a safety management system ([IMO](https://www.imo.org/en/mediacentre/hottopics/pages/autonomous-shipping.aspx)).
- COLREGs are read as reaching unmanned systems ([PACOM TACAID](https://www.pacom.mil/Portals/55/Documents/Legal/J06%20TACAID%20-%20COLREGS%20-%20FINAL.pdf), search extract). A USNI opinion argues a remote operator does not satisfy Rule 5's lookout ([USNI Proceedings](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/2024/february/collision-regulations-need-be-updated-usvs)). Others argue a remote controller may count as master ([Stockton Center](https://digital-commons.usnwc.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3094&context=ils)). This is opinion, not ruling.
- Danish Order no. 1229 of 3 Oct 2023 defines an autonomous vessel as one operating wholly or partly without physical human interaction, covers internal waters, territorial sea and EEZ, and requires authorisation at least four weeks before the voyage ([Order 1229](https://www.dma.dk/Media/638721095895242663/Executive%20Order%20on%20navigational%20safety%20regarding.pdf)).
- The order does not apply to defence activities, including ships under the control of the Danish armed forces ([Order 1229 s.1(4)](https://www.dma.dk/Media/638721095895242663/Executive%20Order%20on%20navigational%20safety%20regarding.pdf)).
- Appendix 2 requires a risk assessment on voyage safety and freedom of navigation, plus area, traffic, warnings, auxiliary vessels, planned voyage and law of the sea compliance ([Order 1229](https://www.dma.dk/Media/638721095895242663/Executive%20Order%20on%20navigational%20safety%20regarding.pdf)).
- The order text names the DMA, but current DMA guidance says DEMA issues the permission, with DMA assessing construction, equipment and operation case by case ([DMA guideline](https://www.dma.dk/Media/638995644980251670/Guideline%20-%20Approval%20process%20for%20Remote%20Operation%20of%20Maritime%20Sea%20Drones.pdf), [DMA page](https://www.dma.dk/safety-at-sea/safety-of-navigation/construction-works-at-sea/navigation-in-connection-with-autonomous-vessels-or-drones-controlled-without-a-fixed-wired-connection)). DMA may ask for a technical description and CONOPS, and a survey needs a separate Geodata Agency permit ([DMA guideline](https://www.dma.dk/Media/638995644980251670/Guideline%20-%20Approval%20process%20for%20Remote%20Operation%20of%20Maritime%20Sea%20Drones.pdf)).
- Activities near traffic routes, routeing systems, anchorages and narrow lanes get extra notice, and inside a VTS area the responsible person must cooperate with VTS ([Order 1229 ss.4, 7](https://www.dma.dk/Media/638721095895242663/Executive%20Order%20on%20navigational%20safety%20regarding.pdf)).
- Under s.8, involved craft must be able to talk on VHF, keep a watch on channel 16, and raise radar visibility if under 12 m ([Order 1229 s.8](https://www.dma.dk/Media/638721095895242663/Executive%20Order%20on%20navigational%20safety%20regarding.pdf)). Breach can bring a fine or up to one year in prison, two in aggravated cases ([Order 1229 s.13](https://www.dma.dk/Media/638721095895242663/Executive%20Order%20on%20navigational%20safety%20regarding.pdf)).
- Saildrone's collision avoidance stops the USV at a safe distance on an intercepting course and holds until the vessel passes, and operators can be called on VHF 16 ([Saildrone](https://www.saildrone.com/news/saildrone-launches-the-future-of-maritime-surveillance-in-the-baltic-sea)).

### Inferences
- The MASS Code does not directly cover a 2-3 m public-service USV, but it gives the right words: remote operations centre, modes of operation, fallback. A commentator's point that fallback must not depend on perfect comms fits the link-loss story ([Maritime Executive editorial](https://maritime-executive.com/editorials/the-mass-code)).
- The scenario's USVs have a camera, an AIS receiver and short-range radio. As specified they are hard to see on a bridge and cannot talk on VHF 16. List this under limits: a real boat needs AIS transmit or radar visibility and a VHF path through shore.
- Pitch line for limits: "This is simulation. A real trial in Danish waters needs permission through the Danish Emergency Management Agency four weeks ahead, or a defence operator."
- Copy Saildrone's simple behaviour for the demo: when a vessel is on an intercepting course, stop and hold. Show it as a delegated safety action.

### Gaps
- COLREG rule text was not verified in this session (fetches refused and search budget spent). From memory, Rule 10(j) and Rule 9(b) say vessels under 20 m shall not impede ships in a traffic lane or narrow channel, Rule 13 makes the overtaking vessel keep clear, and Rule 8 requires a safe distance. Check before quoting.
- No authoritative statement on whether a 2-3 m USV is a COLREG "vessel" under Danish practice.
- The DMA guideline's date. It is undated in the text.

## 6. What makes maritime evidence usable?

### Takeaway
Cases have turned on time-stamped ship data, bridge audio and seabed work, and two major ones were hurt by missing ship data. A USV log can fill part of that gap only if it records raw observations, their source, the time source and who decided what. A hash chain is useful as tamper evidence, not as legal proof of truth.

### Cited Findings
- Eagle S VDR: no usable recording from 18 Dec until 12:59 on 25 Dec, so it missed the EstLink 2 break. It relied on a GPS time signal, fell back to a wrong date such as 2005, then deleted files to free disk. NBI found no signs of tampering ([Helsinki Times](https://www.helsinkitimes.fi/finland/finland-news/domestic/27770-black-box-offline-during-cable-damage-by-russian-tanker-eagle-s.html)).
- After restart it captured the bridge, including the second mate reporting low revolutions at 13:05 ([Helsinki Times](https://www.helsinkitimes.fi/finland/finland-news/domestic/27770-black-box-offline-during-cable-damage-by-russian-tanker-eagle-s.html)).
- The Eagle S evidence list included VHF transcripts, WhatsApp messages, VDR recovery and analysis, ECDIS data, deck log, Finnish Meteorological Institute opinions, NBI statements, Traficom statements, a Kongsberg inspection, standing orders and photographs ([judgment](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-14133-2025-INIT/en/pdf)).
- Yi Peng 3: investigators could not access surveillance images or the VDR, the visit was time-limited, and crew interviews took place with Chinese officials present ([The Record](https://therecord.media/sweden-report-chinese-ship-baltic-sea-cable)).
- Vezhen: interviews, VDR, video and other investigations let prosecutors clear the ship of sabotage ([Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/swedish-prosecutors-confirm-january-cable-break-was-accident-by-bulker)).
- Fitburg: the VDR, released with the file, reportedly captured crew reactions to Coast Guard calls ([Rautio](https://rautiomedia.substack.com/p/finland-is-trying-to-put-a-deniable), secondhand). Turva mapped the drag trail ([Police](https://poliisi.fi/en/-/fitburg-vessel-to-move-to-international-waters-under-border-guard-s-control)).
- Forensics on a severed cable must wait until it is raised ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2024/11/19/baltic-sea-russia-putin-nato-cables/), search extract).
- UNODC says digital evidence must meet authenticity, reliability, integrity and chain of custody tests ([UNODC Annex D](https://www.unodc.org/documents/Maritime_crime/AnnexD_MANUAL_MARITIME_DOMAIN_AWARENESS_AND_DIGITAL_EVIDENCE.pdf), search extract). Boarding training covers evidence transfer and proper handover to police ([UNODC catalogue](https://www.unodc.org/documents/Maritime_crime/UNODC_GMCP_-_Maritime_Law_Enforcement_Training_Catalogue_2024.pdf), search extract).
- Under eIDAS Art 41, a timestamp cannot be refused effect merely because it is electronic or non-qualified, and a qualified timestamp is presumed accurate with data intact ([eIDAS Art 41, UK retained copy](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2014/910/article/41/data.pdf), search extract). It proves when data existed, not who made it ([Namirial](https://focus.namirial.com/en/what-is-a-timestamp/), search extract).
- Denmark has no official definition of electronic evidence ([EJN fiche](https://www.ejn-crimjust.europa.eu/ejnupload/DynamicPages/Fiches%20Belges%20on%20electronic%20evidence%20-%20Denmark.pdf), search extract).
- ICPC says fibre sensing with AIS could give early warnings and supplementary evidence ([ICPC](https://www.iscpc.org/publications/icpc-viewpoints/as-fibreoptic-sensing-in-submarine-cables-develops-rapidly-governments-should-exercise-restraint-in-regulating/), search extract).

### Inferences
**Evidence log, one record per event**
- Record ID, previous-record hash, this-record hash.
- Event type: observation, alert, decision, command, delivery, handover.
- Observation time and logged time, kept separately.
- Time source and quality (GNSS locked, holdover, unknown). This is the Eagle S lesson.
- Observer boat ID, sensor, software version, boat position and its quality.
- Contact ID, estimated position, range, bearing, speed, uncertainty.
- Raw file reference and file hash (image, AIS message).
- AIS association result, with the matching rule and tolerances.
- Rule that fired, with parameters.
- Operator ID, role, decision, stated reason, options on screen, delivery status.
- Standing order version in force.
- Custody: who exported it, when, to whom.

**Hash chain verdict.** Keep it, and describe it modestly. "The log shows if a record was changed after it was written. It does not show the sensor was right." It is cheap to build and easy to demo: edit one record and the check fails. A real unit would add a qualified timestamp at each export. Do not say "court-ready" or "legally admissible"; no source supports that, and admission depends on national law.

**Demo line.** "Three of the five investigations we read turned on who said what, and when. On Eagle S the recorder had no usable data before 12:59. So every observation here carries its time source, and every decision carries a name."

**Retention.** Tie the default to something real: keep at least 90 days, matching the mandatory reporting minimum in Danish VTS guidance, and hold anything marked for investigation.

### Gaps
- Danish criminal rule on free assessment of evidence was not found in a usable source. I believe Danish courts assess evidence freely, but this is unverified.
- UNODC manual's model forms and any AIS preservation procedure were not read (PDF unavailable).
- Data protection rules for images of crew, and whether a coastguard's USV record is police data.
- No case found where a USV record was used in a Baltic prosecution.

## 7. Is immediate start with approve or veto sensible, and how should it be worded?

### Takeaway
Yes, keep immediate start for watching only, because it is lawful, reversible and fast, and the cases show delay is measured in hours. But "veto, never a permission gate" clashes with the brief's "approves decisions". Fix it by having the watch officer approve standing orders up front and approve every step beyond watching. Also add a stand-off, a lane keep-out and a speed check, because the current defaults are unsafe or unrealistic.

### Cited Findings
- First radio contact took 2 h 54 min in Eagle S and about 1 h 22 min in Fitburg, as shown in Table B, and four cables were cut after that first contact in Eagle S ([judgment](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-14133-2025-INIT/en/pdf)).
- Real practice runs from radio to boarding to police lead, with government approval in Eagle S ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/eagle-s-how-yi-peng-3-and-newnew-polar-bear-wreaked-havoc-in-baltic-sea-60-minutes/), [Police](https://poliisi.fi/en/-/eagle-s-tanker-to-move-to-international-waters-under-border-guard-s-control)).
- ICPC asks coast guards to communicate with vessels drifting near cables ([ICPC best practices v1.2](https://www.iscpc.org/documents/?id=3733)).
- The only published shadow distance I found is KV Sortland at 1 nm for about 15 hours ([gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/norwegian-navy-shadows-chinese-ship/)). Danish vessels "loosely" followed Yi Peng 3 ([gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/details-of-baltic-sea-cable-incident-remain-murky-as-danish-navy-shadows-chinese-vessel/)).
- Sample standing orders use more than 1.5 nm CPA in open water and 1.0 nm coastal ([WHOI](https://www.whoi.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/ARM-7.1-Masters-Standing-Orders.pdf), search extract). These are ship-specific, not regulation.
- On 14 Sep 2026 a Russian frigate fired two flares towards a Danish Fennec near Gedser. The minister said there was no warning or radio contact and called the helicopter's distance relatively close but normal, while Russia's ambassador called it provocation ([Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/84541), [Dagens](https://www.dagens.com/europe/russian-frigate-fires-signal-flares-at-danish-military-helicopter-russia-claims-provocation)).
- VTS instructions yield to contradictory safety reasons ([IMO A.1158(32)](https://admin.dma.dk/Media/638539546076830031/A%2032-Res.1158%20-%20GUIDELINES%20FOR%20VESSEL%20TRAFFIC%20SERVICES%20(Secretariat).pdf)). Alert standard timers are 30 s for alarms and 5 minutes for warnings ([MSC.302(87)](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/KnowledgeCentre/IndexofIMOResolutions/MSCResolutions/MSC.302(87).pdf)).
- Current plan: "shadowing, veto available", no approval wait, 3 m/s, 500 m camera footprint, 15 s stale limit, 120 s maximum episode, 30 s veto expiry ([plan copy](current-plan-copy.html)).

### Inferences

**Policy wording to replace the plan's section**

> The watch officer approves the standing orders at the start of the watch. Under them, the nearest eligible USV starts keeping a contact in view at once, from the stand-off ring, and the watch officer is told. Every step beyond watching waits for the watch officer. The watch officer can stop any action at any time, except a safety action.

Short version for judges: **"Watching is pre-approved. Everything beyond watching waits for a person."**

**Who decides what**

| Tier | Action | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic safety, cannot be stopped | Avoid collision, stop and hold, return on reserve, leave the traffic lane | Boat acts, then reports |
| Delegated under standing order | Open a track, keep in view at stand-off, repair sectors, take over relay, log everything, raise alerts | Starts at once. Watch officer may Confirm or Stop |
| Approve, boat holds until a person answers | Close inside stand-off, enter the lane, follow out of the watch area, ask Belt Traffic to hail, mark contact of interest, send a report, release evidence, take a second boat off the cables | No answer means no action. Boat keeps watching |
| Authority only, the system never does this | Order to stop or raise anchor, board, detain, direct to port, contact flag state, assign blame | System can only supply the record |

Add one branch: **possible distress or breakdown**. Hand to JRCC. Vezhen and the ICPC base rate show the innocent explanation is common.

**Default changes**

| Plan default | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Approval is awareness/veto, never a permission gate" | Replace with the tiers above | Brief says the operator approves decisions |
| Acknowledge and Veto buttons | Confirm and Stop on watching; Approve and Decline on holds | Acknowledge means "seen" in real alert systems |
| No stand-off | Stand-off ring 1 nm (1,852 m). Inside it needs approval. Hard floor 500 m as a team default, not doctrine | 1 nm is the only published figure; 500 m echoes the UNCLOS installation zone cap |
| One 500 m camera footprint | Two ranges: detect 2 km, read name or anchor 500 m (synthetic) | Otherwise the stand-off and camera contradict each other, and it shows why closing is a human decision |
| Follow any contact | Eligibility includes speed. If contact is faster than the boat's 3 m/s (5.8 kn), mark "cannot hold" and hand along the track | Yi Peng 3 about 7 kn, Eagle S about 7.6 kn or less |
| 120 s episode, 15 s stale, 30 s veto expiry | Keep only as compressed demo timers, labelled as such. Real values come from a unit's standing orders | Real shadowing ran about 15 h (Sortland) and weeks (Rota). No doctrine supports these numbers |
| Offline: stop after 120 s | Offline: keep watching passively until reserve or a set limit, never escalate, queue the report | Watching needs no fresh permission, and holds stay on hold |
| Warship or state vessel | Keep clear and report only. No auto-follow | Gedser, and the plan's own limits |
| Dark-contact rule only | Keep it, add AIS-on slowdown across a cable corridor | Table A |
| No first-action hint | Suggest "Ask Belt Traffic to hail on VHF 16" | Radio was the first response in Eagle S and Fitburg |

**The anchor look.** The most useful thing a USV can add is an independent answer to the question authorities actually asked: is the anchor up? In Eagle S and Fitburg that was asked by radio, and the reported answer was wrong or disputed. Make "Close to 500 m, photograph both bows" the headline Approve decision, and only where the contact is slow enough to reach. Claim only that it gives a second source, not that it would have prevented damage.

**Pitch wording** (all conditional on the source check above)

Open: "On Christmas Day 2024 the EstLink 2 cable failed at 12:26. Finnish authorities reached the tanker Eagle S at 15:20. According to the indictment, the crew said both anchors were secured. Four more cables were cut before 18:45. No court has yet ruled on guilt; the case is going back to trial."

Update: "On Thursday a Helsinki court jailed two crew of the Fitburg over a drag of about 130 kilometres. The verdict is not final."

Honest turn: "These ships were under way, and only one had a reported AIS gap. The brief's dark loiterer is the case we demo. We also flag ships that slow across a cable."

Close of the demo: "Watching is pre-approved. Everything beyond watching waits for a person. The boats never stop a ship; that is for the Navy, the police and the flag state."

Ask: "We would like to sit with a duty officer from Naval Command or Great Belt VTS and go through the decision cards on synthetic data." The plan's pilot idea stands; this only names candidates, and no contact has been made.

**Never say:** sabotage (unless quoting a court, and then add "not final"), attack, hostile, intercept, detain, hunt, shadow fleet ship (for an unidentified contact), prevented, court-ready, certified, safe distance without a number, proven one-to-five supervision.

**Metrics.** Keep worst revisit age as headline. Add time from contact to first image, and time from alert to watch officer decision, as supporting numbers, because Table B shows response time is what real units struggled with.

**Plan items this resolves.** The [TO CONFIRM] on naming Eagle S can close: the undisputed anchor fall and cable damage are in the judgment. Cite the ruling number rather than the Council link, since that copy's cover is marked LIMITE even though the judgment says the verdict is public. The 30 s approval-wait option should be dropped: it delays lawful watching and fails on link loss, and 30 s in the standard is an audible re-alert, not a wait. Keep the "Gulf of Finland, not Great Belt" caveat.

### Gaps
- No real unit's published stand-off doctrine or rules of engagement. The 1 nm and 500 m defaults must go to the mentors for checking.
- Whether Danish Naval Command would accept automatic start, even for watching. Ask in the first two hours on Friday.
- USV top speed is not in the brief. 3 m/s is the plan's assumption.
- Real minimum distance for reading a ship's name or seeing an anchor chain with a small-boat camera. The 2 km and 500 m ranges are synthetic.
